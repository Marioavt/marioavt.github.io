About Research Teaching

Hi! My name is Mario, I'm from Lima, Peru, and I'm a first-year economics PhD student at UC Berkeley. I'm primarily interested in economic history, international finance, and labor economics.

Previously, I studied economics at Cambridge and economic history at LSE. I then worked for a year as an economic consultant at Cebr.

I'm always open to meeting new people! If you would like to chat, feel free to reach me at mventura [at] berkeley.edu.

My main independent research projects so far have been my two theses.

  • My undergraduate thesis, titled Investigating the fertility decline reversal in high-income economies, built a stylised dynamic model of fertility choice in the context of a closing gender pay gap and slow-moving social norms regarding childcare. I used this to argue that the fertility decline reversal could emerge as a transient response to these norms, which affect the underlying cost of raising children.
  • My master's thesis, titled What drove early Latin American sovereign bond markets? An analysis of the debt of six nascent states, 1825-6, constructed a new dataset of (near-) daily bond prices for six countries involved in the "first Latin American debt crisis". It then combined this data with daily money market reports from the Times newspaper archive to gauge the effect of specific economic and political shocks on investor perceptions and bond prices.

Additionally, I worked as a research assistant for Professor Jason Lennard while at LSE, primarily on projects tied to the financial and macroeconomic history of the UK in the interwar period.
